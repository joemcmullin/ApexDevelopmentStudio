Both platforms

One product, two native apps.

Since September 2026, every Apex app has been planned to ship on both iPhone and Android. Building both natively costs more than a single cross-platform codebase, but it avoids the compromises people notice: scrolling that feels wrong, controls that don't match the system, features that lag behind.

To keep the two apps in step, we write each feature's spec once, then check the Android screens against the iOS reference for layout, color, charts and motion before a release.

Questions

Before you ask.

Do you use a cross-platform framework like Flutter or React Native?

No. We build Android natively in Kotlin and Jetpack Compose, the same way we build iOS natively in SwiftUI. Where it pays off, the two share a Kotlin core for business logic.

Will the Android app look like the iOS app?

It will look like the same product and follow Android's own conventions. We compare every screen against the iOS version so nothing drifts.

Do you handle Google Play submission?

Yes: the store listing, the Data safety form, content rating, test tracks and the staged rollout.

Can the app read health data on Android?

Yes, through Health Connect, with Google Play's health declarations and the permission rationale Play requires.
